# CICIDS2017 source audit checkpoint

Task 8 remains in progress. Acquisition is complete; chronological preparation and benchmarking are not.

The incremental auditor processes 25,000 rows at a time, hashes each source before and after reading, and uses disk-backed full-row duplicate tracking. It records stripped headers, labels, nonfinite numeric values, timestamp parsing failures, ambiguous early-hour timestamps, and backward timestamp steps. Raw files are unchanged. Full-row identity includes the label and does not establish feature-level leakage freedom or detect conflicting labels.

The original audit is preserved at `artifacts/datasets/cicids2017/audit-original.json`. A direct all-column inspection confirmed 288,602 entirely empty records in the Thursday morning web-attack file. These are padding, not malformed network flows. The auditor now counts them separately before validity and duplicate checks; raw row counts still include them. A regression test covers this distinction, including chunks consisting entirely of padding.

The original timestamps have unresolved chronology problems: values such as `6/7/2017 1:00` lack AM/PM, and several files contain backward timestamp steps. The auditor deliberately does not add twelve hours or sort these values. Backward steps alone can reflect export ordering, but sorting cannot resolve ambiguous clock values. The early-hour heuristic is a conservative warning, not proof of the correct time.

Next gate: establish a documented timestamp policy from source evidence, or evaluate a clearly identified regenerated dataset variant. Any change of variant needs its own acquisition receipt, checksums, audit and explicit protocol ruling. Until then, do not create chronological benchmark results from these files. Passing unit tests is evidence about the auditor, not evidence that the dataset is scientifically ready.

Reproduction from the research worktree:

```powershell
python -m experiments.datasets.cicids2017 --data-root '<original checkout>/data/raw/cicids2017/flows' --output artifacts/datasets/cicids2017/audit-padding-aware.json --read-chunk-rows 25000
```

The output must not already exist. A nonzero exit after writing the report means its chronology gate rejected the sources; inspect the report rather than treating rejection as an execution failure.

## Primary-source research and proposed next step

Verification: full suite 95 passed; `git diff --check` clean. The padding-aware audit completed on all eight files and rejected the chronology gate. Thursday morning now reports 458,968 raw records, 288,602 padding records, 1 duplicate flow record, 135 nonfinite flow records, and 170,230 retained records; missing-label and timestamp-parse-failure counts are both zero. Ambiguous clocks and backward steps in other files remain unchanged. The original audit and raw data were preserved.

The correction authors' [2021 tools page](https://intrusion-detection.distrinet-research.be/WTMC2021/tools_datasets.html) directs users to their newer [CNS 2022 study](https://intrusion-detection.distrinet-research.be/CNS2022/index.html), which documents feature-generation and labelling errors and provides recreated datasets. Its [download directory](https://intrusion-detection.distrinet-research.be/CNS2022/Datasets/) currently lists `CICIDS2017_improved.zip` at approximately 328 MB (retrieved 22 September 2026).

This is not a drop-in equivalent of the original export. The authors' [label guidance](https://intrusion-detection.distrinet-research.be/CNS2022/Dataset_Download.html) distinguishes attempted attacks and advises against using them as a separate model class. Their [CICIDS2017 documentation](https://intrusion-detection.distrinet-research.be/CNS2022/CICIDS2017.html) specifies UTC attack intervals and describes packet reordering at the capture stage. Neither finding proves that the downloadable archive resolves our observed CSV clock ambiguity; its actual schema and timestamps still require inspection.

Recommendation, not an implemented protocol change: inspect the regenerated variant as a candidate for the chronological benchmark, keeping the original export and this audit as separate provenance. Before adopting it, explicitly define attempted-attack evaluation treatment and exclude label-derived metadata such as Attempted Category from predictors. Do not merge variants or compare their scores as if their flow populations and labels were identical.
